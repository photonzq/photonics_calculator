Physics notes & assumptions
  • All wavelengths are vacuum wavelengths. In air, $\lambda_{\text{air}} = \lambda_0 / n_{\text{air}}$ with $n_{\text{air}} \approx 1.00027$ at 1550 nm (standard air), i.e. about 0.42 nm shorter; spectrometers and wavemeters often report air values.
  • Inside a medium only $\lambda$ changes ($\lambda = \lambda_0/n$); the frequency $f$ and the photon energy $E = hf$ do not.
  • Photon energy uses the exact SI constants $h = 6.62607015\times10^{-34}$ J·s and $e = 1.602176634\times10^{-19}$ C, so $E[\text{eV}] \approx 1239.842/\lambda_0[\text{nm}]$. The photon flux is $\Phi = P/(hf)$.
  • Band boundaries (UV/VIS/NIR/SWIR/…) follow common usage and differ between standards (e.g. ISO 20473, detector-industry conventions). Telecom bands follow ITU-T: O 1260–1360, E 1360–1460, S 1460–1530, C 1530–1565, L 1565–1625, U 1625–1675 nm.
  • The RGB swatch is an approximate sRGB rendering of a monochromatic line (Bruton's piecewise model), not a colorimetric calculation.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • The three fields use the first-order (linearised) relation $|\Delta f| = c\,\Delta\lambda/\lambda_c^2$, which is what linewidths, FSRs and pulse bandwidths are normally quoted with.
  • The exact width of a band placed symmetrically in wavelength is $\Delta f = c\left(\frac{1}{\lambda_c - \Delta\lambda/2} - \frac{1}{\lambda_c + \Delta\lambda/2}\right) = \frac{c\,\Delta\lambda}{\lambda_c^2 - \Delta\lambda^2/4}$; the relative error of the linear form is $\approx (\Delta\lambda/2\lambda_c)^2$ (0.1 % for 100 nm at 1550 nm). A band symmetric in wavelength is not symmetric in frequency.
  • $\Delta\tilde\nu = \Delta f / c$ is exact (wavenumber is linear in frequency); only the conversion to $\Delta\lambda$ is linearised.

Ultrafast Pulses & Dispersion

⌛ Transform-Limited Pulses

$$ \Delta t_{\text{fwhm}} = \frac{\text{TBP}}{\Delta f} $$
Physics notes & assumptions
  • Durations and bandwidths are FWHM of intensity (temporal and spectral). TBP $= \Delta t\,\Delta f$: $0.4413$ for Gaussian, $0.3148$ for sech².
  • The result is the shortest (transform-limited, unchirped) pulse the spectrum supports; any chirp raises $\Delta t\,\Delta f$ above these values.
  • An autocorrelation FWHM is longer than the pulse: divide by $\sqrt2$ (Gaussian) or $1.543$ (sech²).
  • The centre λ, pulse shape and TBP chosen here are also used by the Pulse Elongation card.

📊 GDD ↔ Total Dispersion ($D$)

$$ \text{GDD} = - \frac{\lambda_c^2}{2 \pi c} D $$
↕
GDD in Femtoseconds Squared
-1,275,448 fs²
Physics notes & assumptions
  • $D$ here is the total (length-integrated) dispersion, e.g. $D_\lambda\,[\text{ps/(nm·km)}] \times L\,[\text{km}]$; GDD $= \beta_2 L = d^2\phi/d\omega^2$.
  • Sign convention: $D > 0$ (anomalous) ⇔ GDD $< 0$. Standard SMF-28 at 1550 nm: $D_\lambda \approx +17$ ps/(nm·km), $\beta_2 \approx -21.7$ ps²/km.
  • The relation is exact for the second-order term only; third-order dispersion (TOD) and higher are not included.

📈 Pulse Elongation (Broadening)

$$ \tau_{\text{out}} = \tau_0 \sqrt{1 + \left( \frac{2\pi \cdot \text{TBP} \cdot |\text{GDD}|}{\tau_0^2} \right)^2} $$
exact for Gaussian · sech² uses the exact numerical curve
Peak Power $P/P_0$
33.9% (-4.7 dB)
Uses λ and TBP from the Transform-Limited card.
Physics notes & assumptions
  • Input pulse assumed transform-limited; linear propagation only (no SPM or other nonlinearity), GDD only (no TOD).
  • Gaussian: the formula above is exact, since $2\pi\cdot0.4413 = 4\ln 2$, i.e. $\tau = \tau_0\sqrt{1+(4\ln2\,\text{GDD}/\tau_0^2)^2}$, and the pulse stays Gaussian, so $P/P_0 = 1/M$. The sign of GDD does not matter for a transform-limited input.
  • sech²: the pulse does not stay sech² under dispersion, and the formula above (with TBP 0.3148) underestimates the FWHM by up to 25 % near $|\text{GDD}| \approx 0.5\,\tau_0^2$. The calculator therefore uses an exact broadening curve $M(|\text{GDD}|/\tau_0^2)$ and peak ratio, tabulated from a numerical (FFT) propagation. The formula is recovered only in the far field, $\tau \to 2\pi\,\text{TBP}\,|\text{GDD}|/\tau_0$, where the temporal profile maps the spectrum. Because the shape changes, $P/P_0$ differs from $1/M$ by up to 11 %.
  • Custom TBP: the Gaussian-form formula with that TBP (approximate; correct far-field limit).
  • When the dispersion is entered as $D$ (ps/nm), it is converted to GDD at the centre λ of the Transform-Limited card.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • Lossless, isotropic, non-magnetic media with real $n_1, n_2$; a single planar interface (no multilayer interference).
  • $R$, $T$ are power (intensity-flux) coefficients; $T = 1 - R$ already contains the $\frac{n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i}$ beam-area factor, so it is not $|t|^2$.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • $\kappa_0$, $\kappa_{\text{ex}}$ and $\kappa$ are energy decay rates (angular); the FWHM linewidth in Hz is $\kappa/2\pi$ and $Q = \omega_0/\kappa = f_0/(\kappa/2\pi)$.
  • $\alpha$ is the power attenuation coefficient: $P(z) = P_0 e^{-\alpha z}$, $\alpha[\text{dB/m}] = 4.343\,\alpha[\text{m}^{-1}]$, and $\kappa_0 = \alpha\, v_g = \alpha c/n_g$. Some papers quote a field coefficient ($\alpha/2$) instead.
  • $\mathcal F = 2\pi/(\alpha L_{\text{rt}})$ and the linewidth formulas assume high finesse ($\alpha L_{\text{rt}} \ll 1$); the round-trip loss field reports the exact $1-e^{-\alpha L}$.
  • Single-mode, traveling-wave resonator side-coupled to one bus waveguide (all-pass configuration), in the temporal coupled-mode theory picture valid for $\kappa \ll$ FSR. Backscattering (mode splitting) and a drop port are not included; a drop port simply adds another $\kappa_{\text{ex}}$ term to $\kappa$.
  • Critical coupling ($\kappa_{\text{ex}} = \kappa_0$) gives zero transmission, $Q_L = Q_0/2$ and build-up $\mathcal F_L/\pi = \mathcal F_0/2\pi$ ($\mathcal F_L$ = loaded finesse). Here "≈ critical" means $|\kappa_{\text{ex}} - \kappa_0| < 2\%\,\kappa_0$.
  • $L$ and $R$ are derived from FSR and $n_g$ ($L = c/(n_g\,\text{FSR})$); $R$ assumes a circular ring.

🪞 Fabry-Pérot (FP) Cavity

$$ \mathcal{F} = \frac{\pi \sqrt{r_{\text{rt}}}}{1 - r_{\text{rt}}}, \quad Q = \frac{2 n_g L}{\lambda_0}\mathcal{F}, \quad r_{\text{rt}} = \sqrt{R_1 R_2}\,e^{-\alpha L} $$
When Finesse/Q edited, solve:
Physics notes & assumptions
  • Plane-parallel (or mode-matched) two-mirror cavity; $R_1, R_2$ are power reflectivities (values above 1 are read as percent) and $\alpha$ is the power loss coefficient of the intracavity medium, so one round trip multiplies the power by $R_1R_2e^{-2\alpha L}$.
  • $r_{\text{rt}} = \sqrt{R_1R_2}\,e^{-\alpha L}$ is the round-trip field factor, and $\mathcal F = \pi\sqrt{r_{\text{rt}}}/(1 - r_{\text{rt}})$ is the exact Airy-function finesse (FSR / FWHM). It approaches $2\pi/(\text{fractional round-trip power loss})$ at high finesse.
  • FSR $= c/(2n_gL)$ uses the group index; the linewidth is FSR$/\mathcal F$ and $Q = f_0/\Delta\nu$.
  • Mirror-phase (penetration-depth) corrections, diffraction loss and Gouy-phase shifts of curved-mirror cavities are not included.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • Phase-matching (Bragg) condition for contra-directional coupling of a mode to itself: $\beta_{\text{fwd}} + \beta_{\text{bwd}} = 2\pi m/\Lambda$, i.e. $m\lambda_B = 2\bar n_{\text{eff}}\Lambda$.
  • $\bar n_{\text{eff}}$ is the length-weighted average of the two section indices, a good approximation for weak index contrast. For strong contrast, use the Bloch-mode effective index from a band-structure simulation.
  • $\kappa$ from the indices: the $m$-th Fourier component of a rectangular effective-index profile is $\Delta n_m = \frac{2|n_1-n_2|}{\pi m}|\sin(\pi m d)|$, and $\kappa = \pi\Delta n_m/\lambda_B$ (so $\kappa = 2\Delta n/\lambda_B$ for $m = 1$, $d = 0.5$). This effective-index estimate ignores the mode-overlap and radiation effects of real corrugations. For strong or deep corrugations, enter $\kappa$ from a simulation (e.g. from the band gap: $\kappa = \pi n_g\,\Delta\lambda_{\text{gap}}/\lambda_B^2$).
  • Uniform, lossless grating in coupled-mode theory, with detuning $\delta = \beta - m\pi/\Lambda \approx -2\pi n_g(\lambda-\lambda_B)/\lambda_B^2$ (first order in $\lambda - \lambda_B$). The spectrum is $R = \frac{\kappa^2\sinh^2(sL)}{\kappa^2\cosh^2(sL) - \delta^2}$ with $s = \sqrt{\kappa^2-\delta^2}$, which continues analytically outside the stop band.
  • $\kappa$ is the field coupling coefficient: $R_{\max} = \tanh^2(\kappa L)$; $\kappa L = 1, 2, 3$ gives 58 %, 93 %, 99 %. The stop band (edges at $|\delta| = \kappa$) sets the width for strong gratings; $\lambda_B^2/(n_g L)$ sets it for weak ones.
  • $L_{\text{eff}} = \tanh(\kappa L)/(2\kappa)$ is the effective (energy-penetration) length of the grating used as a DBR mirror. It enters DBR-cavity FSR as $L_{\text{cav}} + L_{\text{eff},1} + L_{\text{eff},2}$ and tends to $1/(2\kappa)$ for strong gratings.
  • Duty cycle 0.5 with odd $m$ gives the strongest coupling for a rectangular profile; even orders vanish at $d = 0.5$.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • 1D (TE, in-plane) model of a uniform grating. $\bar n_{\text{eff}}$ is the average effective index of the grating region (weighted by the fill factor; Bloch index from simulation if available) and $n_g$ the group index of the grating region. $\theta$ is the fiber angle from the surface normal, measured in the medium of index $n_t$. Because $n\sin\theta$ is conserved across planar layers, a fiber in air above an oxide cladding uses $n_t = 1$ with the angle in air. $\theta > 0$ means the fiber leans toward the propagation direction of the guided wave (forward emission).
  • Phase matching: order $m$ radiates at $n\sin\theta_m = \bar n_{\text{eff}} - m\lambda/\Lambda$ into any medium with $|n\sin\theta_m| < n$. Near-vertical emission ($\theta \approx 0$, i.e. $\Lambda \approx \lambda/\bar n_{\text{eff}}$) coincides with second-order Bragg reflection back into the waveguide, which is why fibers are usually tilted by 8–15°.
  • Bandwidth: at fixed fiber angle, a wavelength change $\Delta\lambda$ shifts the grating's in-plane wavevector relative to the fiber's by $\Delta k_x = 2\pi(n_g - n_t\sin\theta)\Delta\lambda/\lambda^2$. For a Gaussian emitted beam matched to the fiber spot $w_x$ this gives the Gaussian roll-off above, so the 1-dB bandwidth is $2\sqrt{0.1\ln10}\,\lambda^2/[\pi w_x(n_g - n_t\sin\theta)]$. This is the phase-matching limit: it ignores the wavelength dependence of $\alpha$, directionality and back-reflection, so measured bandwidths are usually somewhat smaller.
  • Overlap: a uniform grating emits $|E|^2 \propto e^{-\alpha z}$ from its start ($\alpha$ = power scattering rate). Its best overlap with a Gaussian of radius $w_x$ is 80.1 %, reached at $\alpha w_x \approx 1.36$ with the fiber centred $0.73\,w_x$ from the grating start (for an infinitely long grating; truncating the tail at finite $L$ can raise it slightly, to ≈ 80.5 %, at the cost of unscattered power). The calculator evaluates the overlap numerically for the given $\alpha$ and $L$ and places the fiber optimally. Apodized gratings can exceed 90 %.
  • Peak CE $\approx \eta_{\text{up}}\,(1 - e^{-\alpha L})\,\eta_x$, i.e. directionality × fraction scattered × longitudinal overlap. It assumes perfect transverse (lateral) mode matching, which a 10–12 µm wide grating and taper give for SMF-28, and no back-reflection or Fresnel loss. Typical 220-nm SOI uniform gratings without a bottom mirror reach 60–70 % directionality and −3 to −5 dB CE.
  • $\lambda$ shift per degree is $(d\theta/d\lambda)^{-1}$, handy for tuning the center wavelength with the fiber tilt.

Nonlinear Optics & Soliton Dynamics

🌀 Soliton Parameters & Characteristic Lengths

$$ L_D = \frac{T_0^2}{|\beta_2|} \qquad L_{\text{NL}} = \frac{1}{\gamma P_0} \qquad N = \sqrt{\frac{L_D}{L_{\text{NL}}}} \qquad L_{\text{fiss}} \approx \frac{L_D}{N} $$
Physics notes & assumptions
  • Scalar nonlinear Schrödinger equation with $\beta_2$ and Kerr $\gamma$ only (no loss, TOD, self-steepening or Raman). $T_0$ is the shape parameter: $|A|^2 \propto \text{sech}^2(t/T_0)$ or $e^{-t^2/T_0^2}$.
  • Peak power from energy: $P_0 = E_p/(2T_0)$ for sech², $E_p/(\sqrt\pi\,T_0)$ for Gaussian.
  • Bright solitons need anomalous dispersion ($\beta_2 < 0$). For $\beta_2 > 0$, $N = \sqrt{L_D/L_{\text{NL}}}$ still measures whether SPM or dispersion dominates, but there is no soliton or fission: the pulse broadens and chirps (optical wave breaking at large $N$).
  • $E_1 = 2|\beta_2|/(\gamma T_0)$ is the exact fundamental ($N = 1$) sech soliton energy; for a Gaussian input it is the energy at which $N = 1$, not a soliton solution. An input with $N$ sheds energy and settles to the nearest soliton; order-$N$ solitons have energy $N^2E_1$.
  • $L_{\text{fiss}} \approx L_D/N$ is the usual estimate of the higher-order soliton compression/fission point, valid for $N \gtrsim 3$ (for $N = 1$ nothing happens).
  • $\gamma = n_2\omega_0/(cA_{\text{eff}})$; typical values: SMF-28 ≈ 1.3 W⁻¹km⁻¹, Si₃N₄ waveguides ≈ 1 W⁻¹m⁻¹, LiNbO₃ nanowaveguides ≈ 1 W⁻¹m⁻¹, Si ≈ 100–300 W⁻¹m⁻¹.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • Lugiato–Lefever mean-field model of one mode family of an all-pass resonator: $\kappa \ll$ FSR, integrated dispersion truncated at $D_{\text{int}}(\mu) = D_2\mu^2/2$ (no avoided mode crossings, no $D_3$), no thermal effects, Raman self-frequency shift or photorefraction.
  • $\kappa$ (energy decay rates) as in the Microresonator card; $\eta = \kappa_{\text{ex}}/\kappa$; $v_g = c/n_g$. $P_{\text{th}}$ is the parametric-oscillation threshold at the optimum detuning, equivalent to the familiar $\kappa^2 n^2 V_{\text{eff}}/(8\eta\,\omega_0 c\, n_2)$ with $V_{\text{eff}} = A_{\text{eff}}L$ and $n \to n_g$ (the two forms differ by the choice of $n_{\text{eff}}$ vs $n_g$, an O(10 %) effect).
  • $\delta\omega = \omega_0 - \omega_p > 0$ is the red detuning of the pump from the cold-cavity resonance. Solitons require anomalous dispersion ($D_2 > 0$), bistability ($\zeta_0 > \sqrt3$), and $\zeta_0 < \pi^2f^2/8$. In practice they are reached by a fast laser scan or power kicking because of the thermal triangle.
  • The soliton is $\propto \text{sech}(t/T_0)$ with $T_0 = \frac{1}{D_1}\sqrt{\frac{D_2}{2\delta\omega}} = \sqrt{\frac{-\beta_2 v_g}{2\delta\omega}}$ and FWHM $1.763\,T_0$; its spectral envelope is $\text{sech}^2(\pi T_0\,\Omega/2)$ with a 3-dB width $0.315/\tau_{\text{FWHM}}$.
  • $P_0 = |\beta_2|/(\gamma T_0^2) = 2\delta\omega/(\gamma v_g)$ is the intracavity peak power, and $P_{\text{sol}} \approx \kappa_{\text{ex}}\cdot 2P_0T_0$ is the out-coupled soliton power (pump line excluded, CW background neglected). It is an estimate valid for $f^2 \gg 1$; if it approaches $P_{\text{in}}$ the assumptions have broken down.
  • $\mu_{\text{th}} \approx \sqrt{\kappa/D_2}$ is the mode number of the first (primary) sidebands at threshold, i.e. the Turing-pattern spacing $\mu_{\text{th}}\cdot$FSR.

Physics notes & assumptions

  • $M^2$ uses the embedded-Gaussian picture: $w_0$ and $w(z)$ are second-moment radii of the real beam, and $\theta = M^2\lambda/(\pi n w_0)$. The intensity values assume a TEM₀₀ profile $I = \frac{2P}{\pi w^2}e^{-2r^2/w^2}$ and are only indicative when $M^2 > 1$.
  • $R(z) = z + z_R^2/z$ is smallest ($2z_R$) at $|z| = z_R$; the Gouy phase $\zeta = \arctan(z/z_R)$ goes from $-\pi/2$ to $+\pi/2$ across the focus (×$(1 + 2p + |l|)$ for higher-order LG modes).
  • Power inside radius $r$: $1 - e^{-2r^2/w^2}$ (86.5 % at $r = w$, 98.9 % at $r = 1.5w$); FWHM of intensity $= 1.1774\,w$.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • Ideal thin lens in the paraxial limit (Self's formula); aberrations and lens clipping are ignored, and the lens must be much wider than the beam there (clipping loss at a lens of radius $a$ is $e^{-2a^2/w^2}$).
  • $d_1$ is measured from the input waist to the lens and $d_2$ from the lens to the new waist. Because of diffraction the output waist is not at the geometric image point; for a nearly collimated input ($z_{R1} \gg |d_1 - f|$) it sits at $d_2 \approx f$.
  • $w_2 = w_1 f / \sqrt{(d_1 - f)^2 + z_{R1}^2}$ is maximal for $d_1 = f$ and the $M^2$ factor carries through unchanged ($M^2$ is invariant under ideal lenses).
  • Only converging lenses ($f > 0$) are handled.

Physics notes & assumptions
  • Power coupling between two fundamental (TEM₀₀) Gaussian modes, computed exactly from the complex-$q$ overlap integral at a common plane, in a medium of index $n$ (from the beam inputs) with $\lambda$ the vacuum wavelength. The partial columns isolate each effect: size mismatch alone, size plus axial offset, and lateral offset or tilt alone for a size-matched pair ($w_1 = w_2$), i.e. the alignment tolerances.
  • Special cases: equal waists with lateral offset $\eta = e^{-d^2/w^2}$; equal waists with tilt $\eta = e^{-(\pi n w\theta_t/\lambda)^2}$; the axial formula shown above. The combined result is not simply their product when several are present.
  • Fresnel reflection at the facet (≈ 3.5 % per air–glass surface without AR coating), polarization mismatch and non-Gaussian mode shapes are not included.
  • Beam 1 is taken as a pure Gaussian of waist $w_1$; with $M^2 > 1$ a real beam couples worse into a single-mode fiber (only the TEM₀₀ content couples), so the result is an upper bound.
  • Step-index fiber: $V = 2\pi a\,\text{NA}/\lambda$ (single-mode for $V < 2.405$) and Marcuse's fit $w/a \approx 0.65 + 1.619V^{-3/2} + 2.879V^{-6}$, accurate to ~1 % for $1.2 < V < 2.4$. Datasheet MFDs are measured values and are preferred.
